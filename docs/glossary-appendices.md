# Glossary & Appendices

## Glossary

This glossary provides definitions for key terms used throughout the AI Robotics Humanoid Book. Understanding these terms is essential for comprehending the concepts discussed in the various chapters.

### A

**Actuator**: A component that converts control signals into physical motion, such as a motor or hydraulic cylinder.

**Admittance Control**: A control strategy where the robot's motion is determined by the forces applied to it, creating compliance.

**AI (Artificial Intelligence)**: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems.

**Anthropomorphism**: The attribution of human characteristics or behavior to non-human entities, particularly robots designed to resemble humans.

**Autonomous Robot**: A robot that can operate independently without continuous human control or intervention.

### B

**Balance Control**: Systems and algorithms designed to maintain a robot's stability, particularly important for bipedal robots.

**Behavior-Based Robotics**: An approach to robotics that structures robot control as a collection of simple, reactive behaviors.

**Bipedal Locomotion**: The act of walking on two legs, a key capability for humanoid robots.

**Biomechanics**: The study of the structure, function, and motion of the mechanical aspects of biological systems, applied to robot design.

### C

**Capture Point**: A point on the ground where a biped robot must step to come to a stop, used in balance control.

**Computer Vision**: A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world.

**Control System**: A system that manages, commands, directs, or regulates the behavior of other devices or systems.

**Cognitive Robotics**: A branch of robotics that examines the intersection of robotics and cognitive science.

### D

**Degrees of Freedom (DOF)**: The number of independent movements a mechanical system can make, often referring to robot joints.

**Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning that uses multi-layered neural networks to model and understand complex patterns.

**Dexterous Manipulation**: The ability to perform precise and skillful manipulation tasks, similar to human hand capabilities.

**Distributed Control**: A control architecture where control functions are distributed across multiple processing units rather than centralized.

### E

**Embodied AI**: Artificial intelligence systems that are integrated with physical robots, allowing them to interact with the physical world.

**Emergency Stop**: A safety mechanism that immediately stops robot operation in emergency situations.

**End Effector**: The device at the end of a robot arm that interacts with the environment, such as a gripper or tool.

**Episodic Memory**: A type of memory system that stores specific events or episodes, important for learning robots.

### F

**Force Control**: Control of the forces applied by a robot to its environment, important for safe human-robot interaction.

**Forward Kinematics**: The process of determining the position and orientation of a robot's end effector given the joint angles.

**Fuzzy Logic**: A form of many-valued logic that deals with reasoning that is approximate rather than fixed and exact.

### G

**Gait**: The pattern of movement of the limbs in locomotion, particularly walking patterns in humanoid robots.

**Gaussian Process**: A statistical model used for regression and classification tasks, providing uncertainty estimates.

**General AI**: Artificial intelligence that can understand, learn, and apply knowledge across a wide range of tasks at a human level.

**Gripper**: A device used to grasp and manipulate objects, typically found at the end of a robot arm.

### H

**Haptic Feedback**: The use of touch and motion to communicate with the user, providing tactile information.

**Heuristic**: A problem-solving approach that uses practical methods not guaranteed to be optimal but sufficient for reaching an immediate goal.

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**: The study of interactions between humans and robots, focusing on design and implementation.

**Humanoid Robot**: A robot designed to resemble and interact with humans in a human-like manner.

### I

**Impedance Control**: A control strategy that regulates the dynamic relationship between force and position, creating desired mechanical impedance.

**Inverse Kinematics**: The process of determining the joint angles required to achieve a desired end-effector position and orientation.

**IoT (Internet of Things)**: A system of interrelated computing devices that transfer data over a network without human interaction.

**Intention Recognition**: The ability of a robot to understand human intentions and goals from observed behavior.

### J

**Joint Space**: The space defined by the joint angles of a robot, as opposed to Cartesian space.

**Jacobian Matrix**: A matrix that describes the relationship between joint velocities and end-effector velocities.

**Jerk**: The rate of change of acceleration; important in trajectory planning for smooth motion.

### K

**Kinematics**: The study of motion without considering the forces that cause it, fundamental to robot motion planning.

**Kinesthetic Teaching**: A method of programming robots by physically guiding them through desired motions.

### L

**Legged Locomotion**: The ability to move using legs, a key capability for humanoid robots.

**LiDAR**: Light Detection and Ranging, a remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances.

**Locomotion**: The ability to move from one place to another, a fundamental capability for mobile robots.

**Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)**: A type of recurrent neural network capable of learning long-term dependencies.

### M

**Manipulation**: The ability to handle and control objects in the environment, typically using robotic arms and hands.

**Machine Learning**: A method of data analysis that automates analytical model building using algorithms that iteratively learn from data.

**Mechatronics**: The interdisciplinary field combining mechanical engineering, electronics, computer engineering, and control engineering.

**Middleware**: Software that provides common services and capabilities to applications beyond what's offered by the operating system.

### N

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: A field of AI focused on the interaction between computers and humans through natural language.

**Neuromorphic Computing**: Computing systems that mimic the neural structure and functioning of the human brain.

**Nanorobotics**: The field of creating robots at the nanoscale, though often theoretical for humanoid applications.

### O

**Operational Space Control**: A control framework that allows direct control of task space variables while managing null space motions.

**Optimization**: The process of making a system or design as effective or functional as possible.

**Omni-directional Wheels**: Wheels that allow movement in any direction, useful for mobile robot platforms.

### P

**Path Planning**: The process of determining a route from a starting point to a destination while avoiding obstacles.

**Perception**: The process of acquiring, interpreting, selecting, and organizing sensory information to understand the environment.

**PID Controller**: A control loop feedback mechanism widely used in industrial control systems.

**Probabilistic Robotics**: An approach to robotics that explicitly accounts for uncertainty using probability theory.

### Q

**Q-Learning**: A model-free reinforcement learning algorithm that learns a policy telling an agent what action to take under what circumstances.

**Quaternion**: A mathematical concept used to represent rotations in 3D space, important for orientation control.

### R

**Reinforcement Learning**: A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by taking actions in an environment to maximize cumulative reward.

**Robot Operating System (ROS)**: A flexible framework for writing robot software, providing services for hardware abstraction, device drivers, and message passing.

**ROS 2**: The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved features for real-time and multi-robot systems.

**RRT (Rapidly-exploring Random Tree)**: A motion planning algorithm for high-dimensional spaces that builds a tree of possible paths.

### S

**SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**: The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of an agent's location.

**Sensor Fusion**: The process of combining data from multiple sensors to improve the accuracy and reliability of information.

**Social Robot**: A robot designed to interact with humans in a socially acceptable manner.

**State Estimation**: The process of estimating the internal state of a system from noisy measurements.

### T

**Task Space**: The space in which the task is naturally defined, such as Cartesian space for end-effector position.

**Trajectory Planning**: The process of creating a path that a robot will follow, including position, velocity, and acceleration over time.

**Teleoperation**: The remote operation of a robot by a human operator.

**Tactile Sensors**: Sensors that detect and measure information through touch.

### U

**Unmanned Ground Vehicle (UGV)**: A vehicle that operates without a human crew aboard, including some humanoid robots.

**Uncertainty Quantification**: The process of characterizing and quantifying uncertainties in computational and real-world applications.

### V

**Visual Servoing**: A control strategy that uses visual feedback to control robot motion.

**Virtual Reality (VR)**: A simulated experience that can be similar to or completely different from the real world.

**VSLAM (Visual SLAM)**: SLAM using only visual sensors rather than other types of sensors.

### W

**Whole-Body Control**: A control approach that coordinates all degrees of freedom of a robot simultaneously.

**Wheeled Locomotion**: Movement using wheels, an alternative to legged locomotion.

**Workspace**: The space within which a robot can operate, particularly relevant for manipulator robots.

### X, Y, Z

**Zero Moment Point (ZMP)**: A criterion for static and dynamic stability of legged robots, where the sum of all moments of contact forces equals zero.

**ZMP Controller**: A controller that uses the Zero Moment Point criterion to maintain robot balance.

## Appendices

### Appendix A: Mathematical Foundations

#### Coordinate Systems and Transformations

**Homogeneous Transformation Matrix**:
```
T = [R  p]
    [0  1]
```
Where R is a 3×3 rotation matrix and p is a 3×1 position vector.

**Rotation Matrices**:
For rotations around the x, y, and z axes:
- Rx(θ) = [1   0        0     ]
          [0  cos(θ)  -sin(θ)]
          [0  sin(θ)   cos(θ)]

- Ry(θ) = [ cos(θ)  0  sin(θ)]
          [   0     1    0  ]
          [-sin(θ)  0  cos(θ)]

- Rz(θ) = [cos(θ)  -sin(θ)  0]
          [sin(θ)   cos(θ)  0]
          [  0        0     1]

#### Forward and Inverse Kinematics

**Jacobian Matrix**:
The Jacobian matrix J relates joint velocities to end-effector velocities:
```
v = J(q) * q_dot
```
Where v is the end-effector velocity, q_dot is the joint velocity vector, and J(q) is the Jacobian matrix that depends on the current joint configuration q.

### Appendix C: Safety Standards and Certifications

#### International Standards

**ISO 13482: Personal Care Robots**
- Scope: Safety requirements for personal care robots
- Key requirements: Risk assessment, safety measures, testing procedures

**ISO 10218: Industrial Robots**
- Scope: Safety requirements for industrial robot systems
- Key requirements: Risk reduction, protective measures, information for use

**IEC 61508: Functional Safety**
- Scope: Functional safety of electrical/electronic/programmable electronic safety-related systems
- Key requirements: Safety integrity levels, system development, hardware safety

#### Testing Procedures

**Risk Assessment Process**
1. Hazard identification
2. Risk estimation
3. Risk evaluation
4. Risk reduction
5. Risk evaluation after reduction

**Safety Testing Categories**
- Type testing: Testing of robot design
- Unit testing: Testing of individual robots
- Operational testing: Testing during operation
- Periodic testing: Regular safety inspections

### Appendix E: Sensor Specifications

#### Common Sensors in Humanoid Robots

**Vision Sensors**
- RGB cameras: Standard color cameras for visual perception
- Depth cameras: Provide depth information (e.g., Intel RealSense)
- Stereo cameras: Provide 3D information through binocular vision
- Thermal cameras: Detect heat signatures

**Inertial Sensors**
- Accelerometers: Measure linear acceleration
- Gyroscopes: Measure angular velocity
- Magnetometers: Measure magnetic field for orientation
- IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit): Combined inertial sensors

**Force/Torque Sensors**
- 6-axis force/torque sensors: Measure forces and torques in all directions
- Strain gauge sensors: Measure deformation due to applied forces
- Load cells: Measure weight or force
- Tactile sensors: Detect contact and pressure

**Range Sensors**
- LIDAR: Light Detection and Ranging for precise distance measurement
- Ultrasonic sensors: Use sound waves for distance measurement
- Infrared sensors: Use infrared light for proximity detection
- Time-of-flight sensors: Measure distance using light pulses

### Appendix F: Actuator Specifications

#### Motor Types and Characteristics

**Servo Motors**
- Position control: Precise angular positioning
- Speed control: Controlled rotational speed
- Torque control: Controlled output force
- Feedback: Built-in position/velocity sensors

**Brushless DC Motors**
- Advantages: High efficiency, long life, high power density
- Construction: Electronic commutation instead of brushes
- Control: Requires electronic controller for commutation
- Applications: High-performance robotic joints

**Series Elastic Actuators (SEA)**
- Advantages: Inherent compliance, force sensing capability
- Construction: Spring in series with motor/gearbox
- Applications: Safe human-robot interaction
- Control: Force and position control through spring deflection

#### Performance Metrics

**Torque-Speed Characteristics**
- Continuous torque: Maximum torque sustainable indefinitely
- Peak torque: Maximum torque for short durations
- Speed range: Operating speed range of the actuator
- Power density: Power output per unit mass/volume

**Control Specifications**
- Resolution: Smallest detectable change in position/speed
- Accuracy: How closely the output matches the commanded value
- Bandwidth: Frequency range over which the actuator can respond
- Linearity: How closely the output follows the input

### Appendix G: Communication Protocols

#### Robot Communication Standards

**CAN Bus (Controller Area Network)**
- Purpose: Robust communication in harsh environments
- Characteristics: Differential signaling, message-based protocol
- Applications: Connecting distributed controllers
- Advantages: High reliability, real-time capability

**Ethernet-based Protocols**
- EtherCAT: Real-time Ethernet for control applications
- PROFINET: Industrial Ethernet standard
- Ethernet/IP: Industrial protocol over standard Ethernet
- Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN): Deterministic Ethernet

**Wireless Communication**
- WiFi: High-bandwidth communication for data transfer
- Bluetooth: Short-range communication for devices
- Zigbee: Low-power, low-data-rate wireless networking
- 5G: Next-generation cellular communication for robotics

### Appendix H: Benchmarking and Evaluation Metrics

#### Performance Metrics

**Locomotion Performance**
- Walking speed: Maximum sustainable walking speed
- Energy efficiency: Energy consumption per unit distance
- Stability margin: Measure of balance robustness
- Terrain adaptability: Ability to handle different surfaces

**Manipulation Performance**
- Precision: Accuracy of end-effector positioning
- Dexterity: Ability to perform complex manipulation tasks
- Payload capacity: Maximum weight that can be manipulated
- Workspace: Volume accessible by the manipulator

**Interaction Performance**
- Response time: Time to respond to human commands
- Recognition accuracy: Accuracy of human intention recognition
- Naturalness: How natural the interaction feels to humans
- Safety compliance: Adherence to safety requirements

- DARPA Robotics Challenge: Disaster response scenarios
- EUROC: European Robotics Challenges
